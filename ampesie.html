Ampesie Recipe

Home

Description

Ampesie (or Ampesi) is a classic, highly nutritious traditional Ghanaian meal made of boiled root tubers or plantains served with a rich, savory stew. Unlike banku, there is no fermentation or heavy kneading involved it is straightforward, clean, and comforting. The term Ampesie refers to the boiled element itself, which is most famously paired with Kontomire (Taro/Cocoyam leaves) stew, garden egg stew, or a quick palm oil pepper sauce.

Ingredients

  • Puna Yam: Half a tuber (peeled and sliced into chunks)
  • Green or Apem Plantains: 3 to 4 pieces (peeled or boiled in their skins)
  • Water & Salt: For boiling

For the Kontomire Stew(Palava sauce)

  • Kontomire (Cocoyam/Taro leaves): 2 large bunches (washed and finely chopped)
  • Egusi (Melon seeds): ½ cup (ground into a powder and mixed with 3 tbsp of water to form a paste)
  • Palm Oil: ½ cup
  • Smoked Fish or Momoni: 1 medium smoked mackerel (deboned) and a small piece of momoni (salted fermented fish) for flavor
  • Aromatics: 1 large onion (chopped), 2 cloves of garlic, and a small piece of ginger
  • Pepper: 2–3 kpakposhito (Scotch bonnet or habanero peppers), blended
  • Tomatoes: 2 medium tomatoes (chopped or blended)
  • Boiled Eggs & Pear (Avocado): For serving

Steps

Boiling the ampesie

  1. Prep the Tubers: Cut your peeled yam into thick rounds or wedges and rinse them. Cut off the tips of the green plantains, score the skin lengthwise, and cut them in half.
  2. Boil: Place the yam chunks in a large pot, cover them with water, add a teaspoon of salt, and bring to a boil. After about 10 minutes, add the green plantains to the same pot (they cook slightly faster than yam).
  3. Test: Boil on medium-high heat for another 10–15 minutes. Poke them with a fork if it slides through easily, they are perfectly cooked. Drain the water and set them aside.

    4. Making the kontomire stew

  4. Steam the Leaves: Put your chopped kontomire (or spinach) into a pot with 3 tablespoons of water. Cover and let it steam on medium heat for about 5–7 minutes until wilted and tender. Set aside.
  5. Fry the Base: Heat the palm oil in a saucepan. Drop in your momoni (if using) and let it fry for 1 minute to release its distinct aroma. Add the chopped onions and fry until translucent. Stir in your blended garlic, ginger, and peppers, followed by the chopped tomatoes. Let this simmer for 8–10 minutes until the oil begins to separate.
  6. Add Egusi & Fish: Gently pour your egusi paste into the simmering sauce in small chunks. Do not stir immediately. Cover the pot and let it cook on low heat for 5 minutes so the egusi curdles into beautiful chunks (resembling scrambled eggs). Gently fold in your flakes of smoked mackerel.
  7. Combine: Add the steamed kontomire into the sauce. Stir gently to incorporate everything. Taste and adjust with salt or a stock cube if desired. Let it simmer on low heat for another 5 minutes.

    8. Assemble and Serve

    Plate your hot chunks of boiled yam and plantain. Generously ladle the rich, green Kontomire stew right next to it. Top it off with a hard-boiled egg and a few slices of fresh, creamy avocado pear.
