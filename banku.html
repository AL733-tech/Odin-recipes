Banku Recipe

Home

Description

Banku is a beloved Ghanaian staple dish made from a slightly fermented mixture of corn (maize) and cassava dough cooked in boiling water into smooth, whitish single-serving balls. Because the dough is fermented for a few days before cooking, it has a distinct, pleasantly sour taste and a soft, swallowing texture. It is a cornerstone of Ghanaian cuisine, particularly popular among the Ga, Ewe, and Fante people, but enjoyed nationwide.

Ingredients

  • Fermented Corn Dough: 2 cups
  • Fermented Cassava Dough: 1 cup (Rule of thumb: use a 2:1 ratio of corn to cassava)
  • Water: 2 to 3 cups (plus extra on standby)
  • Salt: 1 teaspoon (to taste)

Steps

  1. Dissolve the Dough: Put the corn and cassava dough into a clean pot. Pour in the water a little at a time, using your clean hands or a whisk to thoroughly mash and dissolve any lumps. The mixture should form a smooth, watery paste (similar to a thick pancake batter). Stir in the salt.
  2. Start Cooking (The Stirring Phase): Place the pot over medium heat. Using a strong wooden paddle (traditionally called a banku tswi), begin stirring the mixture continuously. Do not stop stirring, or lumps will form immediately.
  3. Knead the Banku (The "Driving" Phase) As the mixture heats up, it will quickly begin to thicken and harden. Once it becomes a solid paste, change your motion from stirring to kneading. Use the wooden paddle to press the dough against the sides of the pot repeatedly. This process is locally called "driving" the banku.
  4. Add Water to Steam After about 10–15 minutes of heavy kneading, the dough will turn from a bright white to an off-white, slightly translucent color. At this point, create a few holes in the dough with your paddle, pour about ½ cup of hot water around it, cover the pot with a lid, and let it steam on low heat for 5 minutes.
  5. Final Knead: Remove the lid and use your paddle to thoroughly mix the water back into the dough. Knead vigorously for another 3–5 minutes until the banku is perfectly smooth, stretchy, and uniform in texture.
  6. Shape and Serve Wet a small, deep bowl with a little water so the dough doesn't stick. Scoop a portion of the hot banku into the bowl and shake it in a circular motion until it forms a perfectly smooth, shiny ball. Repeat for the remaining dough.
